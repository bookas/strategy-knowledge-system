## Для чего существует эта глава

Стратегия становится практически значимой только тогда, когда помогает принимать реальные решения о возможностях. Эта глава исследует, как организация должна решать: входить ли в новый рынок, проект, инвестицию, партнёрство, продукт или направление; отказываться; откладывать решение ради знания; либо признать, что новая возможность ставит под вопрос саму действующую Strategy.

Глава должна научить не механически ставить галочку «strategic fit», а различать привлекательность возможности, соответствие стратегии, способность организации действовать, opportunity cost, uncertainty, reversibility, downside, learning value и последствия для уже сделанных commitments. Итогом должна стать исследовательски обоснованная архитектура решения, а не заранее заданный бинарный чек-лист.

Research task: independently determine how strategic opportunities should be evaluated and how decision boundaries can translate Strategy into repeatable opportunity-selection decisions. Do not assume that GO/NO-GO is binary, that strategic fit dominates all other criteria, or that deviation from current Strategy is automatically invalid. Test competing lenses: simple rules, strategic decision process, opportunity evaluation, real options, bounded rationality, escalation/exit, strategic renewal and emergent opportunity.

Incoming context: RQ-01–RQ-05 establish Strategy as plural, object-dependent, bounded, and choice-related; RQ-05 distinguishes constraint/choice, trade-off/priority, commitment/optionality, not-now/never. # Layer 0 — Chapter Passport & Research Specification

## 1. Place and purpose

RQ-06 follows RQ-05 Choice, Scarcity & Trade-offs and precedes RQ-07 Diagnosis & Starting Point. It is the bridge between Strategy as a system of choices and Strategy as a system that governs concrete opportunities. It also creates the explicit handoff to the cross-cutting artifact Strategy-to-Execution Decomposition / Work Package after a GO decision.

## 2. Central Research Question

How should an organization determine whether a concrete opportunity deserves commitment, rejection, deferral, further learning, or reconsideration of the Strategy itself?

## 3. Research goal

Build a defensible conceptual architecture for strategic opportunity selection without assuming a universal scoring matrix. Establish what information belongs in the decision, how Strategy should constrain but not imprison choice, what role rules and judgment play, how uncertainty changes the decision form, and what decision outcomes are conceptually distinct.

# 2. Reference Reconnaissance

The initial reference scan identifies five useful traditions rather than one established 'Go/No-Go theory'. This is itself important: the business phrase Go/No-Go appears to be an applied decision label, while the underlying theory is distributed across strategic decision processes, simple rules, opportunity evaluation, real options and behavioral decision research.

| **Lens** | **Core references** | **What it contributes** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple rules | Eisenhardt & Sull; Bingham/Eisenhardt tradition | Boundary/selection, priority, timing and stopping rules for recurring opportunity-rich environments. |
| Strategic decision process | Mintzberg, Raisinghani & Théoret; bounded rationality tradition | Strategic decisions are iterative, interrupted and not reducible to one linear rational screen. |
| Opportunity evaluation | Wood & Williams | Rule-based evaluation; opportunity cues and decision-maker knowledge alter perceived attractiveness. |
| Real options | Bowman & Hurry; McGrath | Decision may be about preserving, exercising or abandoning an option and staging commitment under uncertainty. |
| Behavioral/organizational | Staw; strategic-choice/power lenses | Commitment, responsibility, politics and interpretation can bias both entry and exit. |

# 3. Research Pass 1 — Constructing the Decision Architecture

## 3.1 Go/No-Go is not a strategy type

The first conceptual correction is terminological. Go/No-Go is not a separate type of Strategy comparable to corporate, business or functional strategy. It is a strategic decision mechanism: a way to translate an existing strategic logic into a verdict about a concrete opportunity. It may itself contain rules, but the rules are subordinate to or constitutive of a broader strategic system depending on context.

Therefore RQ-06 asks not 'what is the Go/No-Go strategy?' but 'what makes a strategic opportunity-selection decision valid, repeatable and revisable?'

## 3.2 The decision object must be explicit

An opportunity can mean a market, customer segment, partnership, acquisition, product, technology, grant, investment, project or experiment. These objects differ in irreversibility, evidence needs and authority. A universal form that treats all of them identically risks false precision. The first step is therefore classification of the decision object and the commitment it would create.

A useful distinction is between opportunity existence, opportunity attractiveness, organizational appropriateness and commitment decision. Wood & Williams show that evaluators use rules concerning cues such as novelty, resource efficiency and worst-case scenarios, and that evaluator knowledge changes how those cues are weighted. This supports separating 'the opportunity looks attractive' from 'this organization should act now'.

## 3.3 Fit is necessary information, not an automatic verdict

Strategic fit asks whether an opportunity reinforces current direction, choices, capabilities or activity system. It is useful because a locally attractive project can dilute a coherent Strategy. But a fit-first filter can become circular: Strategy says what we do; therefore only what we already do fits; therefore Strategy can never change.

RQ-01 and RQ-10's future adaptation problem require a second question: does the opportunity merely violate Strategy, or does it provide credible evidence that Strategy's assumptions, boundaries or opportunity set have changed? The same 'misfit' can therefore imply NO-GO in one case and STRATEGY REVIEW in another.

Strong fit analysis must identify which strategic element is implicated: purpose/mission constraint, explicit strategic choice, capability architecture, resource commitment, sequencing assumption, risk boundary, or merely an old plan. RQ-04 prevents treating all these as equivalent.

## 3.4 Keep decision dimensions separate before combining them

| **Dimension** | **Question** | **Common category error** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic fit | Does this belong with our current strategic logic? | Treating fit as proof of attractiveness. |
| Value/desirability | Would success create meaningful value/outcome? | Treating high upside as reason we are the right actor. |
| Evidence | What do we actually know? | Converting confidence into fact. |
| Feasibility | Can it technically/institutionally be done? | Confusing possible with strategically wise. |
| Capability/readiness | Can we execute or deliberately build what is missing? | Rejecting every capability gap as NO-GO. |
| Opportunity cost | What better use of capacity is displaced? | Comparing opportunity only with doing nothing. |
| Timing | Why now; what window opens/closes? | Assuming good opportunity is good at any time. |
| Downside/exposure | What loss, lock-in or second-order effect is possible? | Averaging catastrophic downside into an attractive score. |
| Reversibility | Can commitment be reduced/reversed? | Treating pilot and full-scale entry as same decision. |
| Learning value | Can a smaller action materially reduce uncertainty? | Using 'pilot' as excuse for endless experimentation. |

This separation is critical. A weighted score can be useful for comparison, but collapsing all dimensions too early allows a very high score on one dimension to compensate for a non-compensable boundary violation or catastrophic downside.

## 3.5 Simple rules: a serious candidate mechanism

Eisenhardt and Sull's simple-rules tradition is unusually close to the practical problem. Their work describes rules that define boundaries/selection, priorities, timing and stopping/exit conditions. In opportunity-rich, fast-moving settings, a small number of rules can guide decentralized action without a detailed prediction of every future case.

Later research distinguishes selection rules that specify which opportunities to choose from procedural rules that guide execution. This is an important interface for our architecture: RQ-06 should own selection logic; the Work Package artifact should not inherit responsibility for deciding whether the opportunity belonged in Strategy in the first place.

However, rules must be tailored to a defined recurring process. 'Only do strategic projects' is not a useful boundary rule because it simply restates the problem. Good rules must discriminate observable cases and leave appropriate judgment.

## 3.6 Priority and opportunity cost

RQ-05 established that a positive business case is not enough; opportunity cost matters. RQ-06 turns this into a decision requirement. An opportunity should be compared with the best credible alternative uses of the same scarce capacity, not merely against zero. This also means decisions can change as the opportunity portfolio changes.

Priority rules are therefore not secondary. If ten opportunities all pass a boundary test but the organization can pursue two, selection requires comparative logic. This is where a binary Go/No-Go vocabulary becomes inadequate: 'valid' and 'funded now' are distinct statuses.

## 3.7 Real options: the verdict can concern commitment size

Bowman & Hurry and McGrath challenge binary commitment. Under uncertainty and irreversibility, an organization may rationally acquire or preserve an option, make a small positioning investment, wait for information, then exercise or abandon. The strategic question becomes: what is the smallest commitment that preserves meaningful upside while buying decision-relevant learning?

This creates a strong theoretical basis for a state such as LEARN / OPTION, but only with boundary conditions. Adner & Levinthal warn against labeling ordinary staged projects as 'real options' when there is no meaningful future discretionary choice. Therefore a pilot is not automatically an option; it must produce information capable of changing a later commitment decision.

## 3.8 Worst-case and non-compensable boundaries

Wood & Williams' empirical opportunity-evaluation work is useful because worst-case scenario can reduce the positive influence of novelty or resource efficiency. Strategically, this suggests that some dimensions should not be averaged away. Legal, ethical, existential, mission-critical or unacceptable downside boundaries may be non-compensable.

But RQ-06 must avoid inventing a universal list of hard boundaries. Their source depends on the strategic object and higher-order architecture. A boundary is valid only if its authority and rationale are known; otherwise 'non-negotiable' can become executive preference disguised as Strategy.

## 3.9 Decision states — first-pass hypothesis

The evidence supports testing more than GO/NO-GO. A provisional taxonomy is:

| **State** | **Meaning** | **Not the same as** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GO | Commit at the approved scale now. | 'Good idea' or unlimited commitment. |
| CONDITIONAL GO | Commit only if explicit prerequisite/threshold is satisfied. | Vague optimism. |
| LEARN / OPTION | Make a bounded action whose primary purpose is to preserve option or reduce decision-relevant uncertainty. | Endless pilot. |
| NOT NOW | Opportunity may fit, but timing/capacity/evidence does not justify current commitment. | Permanent rejection. |
| NO-GO | Do not pursue under current strategic boundaries; rationale is substantive. | Low priority. |
| STRATEGY REVIEW | Opportunity conflicts with current Strategy but evidence is strong enough to question the Strategy/assumption rather than simply reject the opportunity. | Automatic exception. |

This taxonomy is a pedagogical synthesis and must survive Pass 2 before adoption.

## 3.10 Authority, override and exceptions

A decision architecture is incomplete without authority. Who may approve GO, define a hard boundary, override it, or reopen NO-GO? RQ-03's agency work applies directly. A decentralized opportunity process may use simple rules, but exceptions need an escalation path. Otherwise either local actors silently violate Strategy or top management becomes a bottleneck for every case.

An override should therefore be visible as an override, not retroactively reclassified as 'fit'. It should record what boundary was crossed, why, who authorized it, whether the case is exceptional, and whether repeated exceptions indicate that Strategy itself needs review.

## 3.11 Decision record and learning

A strategic decision should leave enough contemporaneous evidence to learn later: what opportunity was believed to exist; key assumptions; alternatives; boundaries; evidence; uncertainty; expected outcomes; commitment scale; reasons for rejection/deferral; and what future evidence would reopen or stop the decision. Without this, retrospective success or failure invites hindsight bias and organizational mythology.

This is an interface with Strategic Knowledge / Organizational Memory, not a deep documentation specification here.

## 3.12 Handoff after GO

A GO is not execution. It authorizes a bounded commitment and must hand off the strategic rationale to Strategy-to-Execution Decomposition / Work Package. The minimum handoff is: strategic reason, intended outcome, approved scope/commitment, non-negotiable boundaries, assumptions to test, dependencies, owner/authority and review/stop conditions. Detailed Work Package structure remains deferred.

# 4. First-Pass Working Model

A first-pass decision flow can be expressed as a sequence of questions, not a universal algorithm:

1. What exactly is the opportunity and what commitment is being requested?

2. What strategic object and decision authority are involved?

3. Which current strategic choices/boundaries are relevant?

4. Is the opportunity valuable/attractive independently of fit?

5. What evidence supports the opportunity and what remains uncertain?

6. Can the organization execute, partner, or deliberately build the missing capability?

7. What alternative use of capacity is displaced?

8. What downside is non-compensable or difficult to reverse?

9. Can a smaller commitment buy decision-relevant learning or preserve an option?

10. Does misfit mean reject the opportunity, or does it challenge an assumption of the Strategy?

11. Which decision state is justified, at what scale, and under whose authority?

12. What evidence will trigger continuation, escalation, stop, reopening or Strategy Review?

This is deliberately not a weighted score. Pass 2 attacks it.

# 5. Research Pass 2 — Independent / Adversarial Re-run

## 5.1 Attack: real strategic decisions are not clean gates

Mintzberg, Raisinghani & Théoret's study of 'unstructured' strategic decisions describes iterative identification, development and selection with interruptions, cycling and branching. This weakens any implication that opportunity evaluation is a single front-end gate. A strategic verdict may emerge through search, design, bargaining and repeated authorization.

Revision: RQ-06 should describe decision architecture, not promise a universally linear process.

## 5.2 Attack: rules can become stale

Simple rules gain value from parsimony and repeatability, but the same strength creates risk. Rules learned from past opportunities may encode obsolete environments or local experience. Eisenhardt/Sull explicitly emphasize a small context-specific set rather than bureaucracy. Therefore boundary rules themselves need review triggers; repeated high-quality exceptions are evidence against the rule.

Revision: rules are hypotheses about recurring decisions, not sacred Strategy.

## 5.3 Attack: strategic fit can suppress renewal

A strict fit test selects opportunities similar to current Strategy and systematically filters weak signals of discontinuity. This is particularly dangerous where Strategy is based on assumptions that new evidence is invalidating. Emergent-strategy and dynamic-capability lenses therefore strengthen STRATEGY REVIEW as a distinct state.

Revision: every major NO-GO caused by misfit should distinguish 'opportunity violates a still-valid boundary' from 'opportunity provides evidence boundary may be obsolete'.

## 5.4 Attack: opportunity evaluation is evaluator-dependent

Wood & Williams show that opportunity cues interact with evaluator market and technology knowledge. Thus the same opportunity can receive different rational-seeming evaluations because decision rules and knowledge differ. This undermines the fiction of a purely objective score.

Revision: decision records should identify material judgment calls and knowledge gaps; for high-stakes cases, diverse expertise or independent challenge may improve robustness.

## 5.5 Attack: politics and authority

Strategic decisions occur inside power structures. A rule can be technically coherent yet function as a political device to privilege one unit's opportunities. RQ-03 already warned that authority and object differ. RQ-06 therefore cannot treat governance as an administrative afterthought.

Revision: ownership of criteria, authority to change them and transparency of overrides belong inside the decision architecture.

## 5.6 Attack: 'learn' can hide avoidance

Real-options reasoning is often overextended. If a pilot does not create a meaningful future choice or decision-relevant information, it is not a strategic option; it may simply postpone commitment. Likewise, repeated learning actions consume scarce capacity.

Revision: LEARN / OPTION requires an explicit uncertainty, a test capable of reducing it, a bounded cost/time, and a predeclared next decision.

## 5.7 Attack: NO-GO can be temporary or domain-limited

RQ-05 already distinguished Never from Not Now. RQ-06 strengthens the need to attach scope and expiry to decisions. A NO-GO for one geography, scale, partner or business unit need not be universal. Conversely, a hard exclusion should identify the higher-order boundary that makes it hard.

Revision: every verdict requires scope, scale and temporal validity.

## 5.8 Attack: one score destroys non-compensable logic

Multi-criteria scoring can support comparison but may allow high upside to offset an unacceptable boundary violation or catastrophic exposure. It also creates false precision when inputs are uncertain.

Revision: use a layered architecture: hard/qualifying boundaries first; then comparative dimensions; then judgment. Do not automatically sum all dimensions.

## 5.9 Attack: opportunity framing precedes evaluation

A badly framed opportunity can pass a sophisticated filter. For example, evaluating a proposed solution rather than the underlying problem can make all criteria internally consistent but strategically irrelevant. This is a direct handoff to RQ-07 Diagnosis.

Revision: RQ-06 requires a minimal framing check but must not duplicate diagnosis methodology.

## 5.10 Pass-2 verdict on decision states

| **State** | **After adversarial pass** | **Boundary condition** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GO | Retain | Always specify scale/scope and review conditions. |
| CONDITIONAL GO | Retain | Condition must be observable and decision-relevant. |
| LEARN / OPTION | Retain, strengthened | Must create real future discretion/information; bounded test. |

# 6. Revised Decision Architecture after Pass 2

The strongest synthesis is layered rather than linear:

| **Layer** | **Question** | **Output** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Frame | What opportunity/problem and requested commitment are we actually evaluating? | Decision object + scope. |
| 1. Authority & hard boundaries | Who decides; what cannot be compensated away? | Eligible / escalation / immediate NO-GO. |
| 2. Strategic relationship | Reinforce, stretch, conflict with, or challenge current Strategy? | Fit classification, not verdict. |
| 3. Attractiveness & evidence | Value, evidence, timing, downside, uncertainty. | Opportunity case. |
| 4. Organizational ability | Existing/buildable/partnerable capabilities and capacity. | Readiness/gap logic. |
| 5. Alternatives | What is displaced; what portfolio interaction occurs? | Opportunity cost / priority. |
| 6. Commitment design | Full commitment, staged option, condition, defer? | Scale and reversibility. |
| 7. Verdict & governance | Which state, who authorizes, what override? | GO/Conditional/Learn/Not Now/No-Go/Review. |
| 8. Learning & handoff | What must be recorded and what happens next? | Decision record or execution handoff. |

The model deliberately separates 'fit' from 'verdict'. This is the most important architectural conclusion of RQ-06.

# 8. Antipatterns & Failure Modes

| **Failure mode** | **Mechanism** | **Consequence** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fit theatre | Criteria merely restate current Strategy. | No learning; Strategy becomes self-sealing. |
| Score worship | Uncertain judgments are collapsed into precise totals. | False objectivity; hard boundaries compensated away. |
| Opportunity seduction | Large upside dominates fit, capacity and opportunity cost. | Opportunistic drift. |
| Strategy prison | All nonconforming opportunities are rejected. | Renewal and weak signals suppressed. |
| Pilot purgatory | LEARN repeats without next decision. | Capacity consumed; no commitment. |
| Exception creep | Overrides become normal but Strategy is never updated. | De facto Strategy diverges from declared Strategy. |
| Executive veto without rule | Personal preference masquerades as strategic boundary. | Low legitimacy and learning. |
| No owner | Decision criteria exist but authority is unclear. | Delay, bargaining, inconsistent outcomes. |
| Sunk-cost GO | Past investment becomes reason for more investment. | Escalation of commitment. |
| Permanent NO-GO | Decision has no scope/expiry despite changing conditions. | Useful opportunities remain excluded. |
| No decision record | Rationale and assumptions vanish. | Hindsight bias; no organizational learning. |
| GO without handoff | Opportunity approved but strategic logic is lost in projectization. | Execution optimizes deliverables rather than strategic outcome. |

# 10. Interface: Strategy-to-Execution Decomposition / Work Package

- The six-state decision taxonomy is our synthesis, not established consensus.

- The layered decision architecture is pedagogical synthesis; empirical validation as a complete model is absent.

- Hard vs soft boundary governance needs deeper linkage to Principles/Governance later.

- Strategic fit remains under-theorized until Coherence RQ-09.

- Evidence thresholds and validation methods remain intentionally deferred.

- Portfolio-level selection and capital allocation are only interfaces here.

- Decision politics are acknowledged but not deeply researched.

- A repeated exception may imply Strategy Review, but the threshold for 'repeated' is not universal.

## 11.8 Duplication review

No harmful duplication detected. RQ-05 explains the nature of strategic choice; RQ-06 explains the governance of a concrete opportunity. RQ-07 will investigate diagnosis/problem framing, not re-score opportunities. Strategic Sequencing will govern stages after or within commitment; Work Package will translate authorized commitment into execution.

## 11.9 Self-review verdict

RQ-06 is valid as an independent Core chapter. The strongest finding is that strategic opportunity selection should not be modeled as a single weighted Go/No-Go score. A more defensible architecture separates hard boundaries, strategic relationship, attractiveness/evidence, organizational ability, alternatives/opportunity cost, commitment design, governance and learning. Strategic fit informs the decision but cannot be allowed to make Strategy self-sealing.

The provisional six-state model survives adversarial review as a useful teaching device: GO, CONDITIONAL GO, LEARN/OPTION, NOT NOW, NO-GO, STRATEGY REVIEW. It must remain explicitly identified as synthesis rather than academic taxonomy.

# 15. Final Synthesis

Strategic Go/No-Go is best understood not as a separate strategy type and not as a binary gate, but as a decision architecture that translates Strategy into governed commitments. The decision must distinguish whether an opportunity is valuable, whether it belongs with current Strategy, whether the organization can act, what alternative capacity it displaces, what uncertainty can be reduced, what downside is non-compensable, and how much commitment is justified now.

The central correction to common practice is: strategic fit is an input, not the verdict. A Strategy that rejects every misfit becomes incapable of renewal; a Strategy that accepts every attractive opportunity ceases to constrain action. The architecture therefore needs both boundaries and a legitimate route for challenging the boundaries.

After two research passes, six decision states remain useful as a pedagogical synthesis: GO, CONDITIONAL GO, LEARN/OPTION, NOT NOW, NO-GO and STRATEGY REVIEW. Their value lies in separating different reasons not to make full commitment now. Every verdict should have scope, scale, temporal validity, authority, rationale and conditions for reopening or stopping.

A GO ends RQ-06 and begins a different problem: translating authorized strategic intent into executable work. That handoff is now explicitly assigned to Strategy-to-Execution Decomposition / Work Package.
