# Part 01 · RQ-09

# STRATEGIC COHERENCE & ALIGNMENT

When do good choices become one strategy?

</div>

## Для чего существует эта глава

Предыдущие главы позволили диагностировать стратегическую ситуацию, сделать выбор и сформулировать причинную логику отдельных решений. Но организация может иметь несколько разумных решений, каждое из которых по отдельности выглядит обоснованным, и всё же не иметь целостной Strategy. RQ-09 исследует, когда набор choices, commitments, capabilities, activities и causal mechanisms образует совместимую и взаимно поддерживающую систему, а когда — лишь портфель хороших, но разрозненных идей.

Глава должна научить различать consistency, fit, complementarity, alignment и coherence; видеть конфликты между локально рациональными решениями; понимать ценность взаимного усиления — и одновременно не превращать 'всё должно быть согласовано' в догму, которая блокирует exploration, modularity, ambidexterity и стратегическое обновление.

## AI Research & Review Annotation

Research task: determine whether Strategic Coherence is a distinct and decision-useful construct, what relationships among strategic elements it describes, what degree of consistency/fit/reinforcement is actually required, and when tension, modularity or partial incoherence can be rational. Do not assume that more alignment is always better or that Porter's activity-system fit is universal outside competitive business strategy.

Incoming findings: RQ-01 treats Strategy as direction/choice/configuration/adaptation; RQ-03 separates object, actor and authority; RQ-04 distinguishes Strategy from its representations/implementation; RQ-05 establishes trade-offs, commitments and option-set effects; RQ-06 shows a locally attractive opportunity can still conflict with Strategy; RQ-08 distinguishes the causal quality of individual strategic arguments. RQ-09 must ask whether multiple arguments and choices can coexist and reinforce one another at the level of the strategic system.

Competing hypotheses: H1 Strong Fit — high-quality Strategy requires mutually reinforcing choices; H2 Minimum Consistency — absence of material contradiction may be sufficient; H3 Modular Coherence — several internally coherent modules can coexist with weak coupling; H4 Adaptive Tension — deliberate inconsistency/tension can preserve exploration and renewal; H5 Contingent Fit — coherence has no universal form and depends on environment/object/time horizon.

Mandatory research sequence: Starting Position → Main RQs → Reference Reconnaissance → Pass 1 → independent adversarial Pass 2 → Pass 3 Concept Destruction / Lock-in Audit → Transfer/Causal/Contradiction/Concept audits → Claim Ledger → Cumulative Self-Review → Chapter Exit Gate. Pass 3 must actively attack locked positions, tautologies, generic language, hidden normativity, construct redundancy, unfalsifiability and over-integration.

## AI audit specification

- Ask whether 'coherence' adds explanatory/decision value beyond fit, consistency, alignment and complementarity.

- Reject definitions that explain coherence by saying elements are 'coherent'.

- Require a specified object: coherence of what with what, for which actor, level, horizon and outcome?

- Seek minimal counterexamples to every strong claim.

- Test whether a supposedly coherent system is simply rigid/path-dependent.

- Test whether apparent incoherence is deliberate separation, option preservation or ambidexterity.

- Distinguish local fit from organization-level/global performance.

- Check transfer from competitive activity systems and manufacturing complementarities before universalization.

- After Pass 3 retain only claims with discriminating power.

# Starting Position — Initial Filling

- RQ-08 makes individual action→outcome arguments criticizable, but does not tell whether several mechanisms are mutually compatible.

- RQ-05 and RQ-06 imply that choices consume shared capacity and can alter future options, creating cross-choice effects.

- Porter suggests strategy-specific activity fit can produce reinforcement and sustainability, but his claim is embedded in competitive business strategy.

- Configuration and complementarity research suggests performance can depend on systems of interdependent elements rather than isolated practices.

- Exploration/exploitation and ambidexterity research suggests complete alignment may be dangerous or impossible.

- Current working hypothesis: coherence is not maximum similarity or uniformity; it concerns whether material interdependencies among strategic elements are compatible with the strategic logic at the relevant level and horizon.

- This hypothesis must be attacked in Passes 2 and 3.

# 3. Research Pass 1 — Building the Coherence Construct

## 3.1 Why individual quality is not system quality

Suppose each strategic initiative has a plausible causal argument. It does not follow that the portfolio of initiatives is jointly rational. Two initiatives may compete for the same scarce capability, require contradictory customer promises, impose incompatible operating models, or create incentives that cancel each other's mechanism. RQ-09 therefore introduces a system-level question: what changes when decisions interact?

The key shift is from evaluating elements to evaluating relations among elements.

## 3.2 Consistency is the weakest useful form

Porter's activity-system argument distinguishes simple consistency from stronger reinforcement and optimization. This is analytically useful even if not universal. Consistency means one choice does not materially undermine another or the intended position. It is a floor, not necessarily a source of advantage.

Example in abstract form: a low-cost promise and an activity that systematically raises unit cost without a compensating mechanism are inconsistent. But two activities can be consistent while producing no special joint benefit.

## 3.3 Complementarity and reinforcement

Milgrom & Roberts give a more formal meaning to systems effects: choices can be complements when increasing one raises the returns to increasing another. This matters because the value of a strategic element may depend on the configuration around it. The relevant question becomes not only 'is A good?' but 'what is A worth given B, C and D?'

Porter's reinforcement is related but not identical to economic complementarity. RQ-09 should not collapse these traditions into one concept.

## 3.4 Coherence as relational quality

Pass 1 proposes a working definition: Strategic Coherence is the degree to which material relationships among strategic choices, commitments, capabilities, resource allocations and causal mechanisms are compatible with — and where relevant reinforce — the strategic logic of the focal actor at a specified level and time horizon.

The definition deliberately includes 'where relevant'. It does not require every element to reinforce every other element. It also requires a focal actor/level/horizon, preventing coherence from becoming an aesthetic judgment.

## 3.5 Fit is multidimensional

| **Relation** | **Core question** | **Failure if absent** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choice↔Choice | Do commitments contradict or reinforce each other? | Strategic contradiction / dilution. |
| Choice↔Capability | Can required capabilities support the choice or be built? | Unexecutable logic. |
| Choice↔Resource allocation | Do resources follow priorities? | Declared Strategy without commitment. |
| Mechanism↔Mechanism | Can causal mechanisms operate simultaneously? | One initiative neutralizes another. |
| Strategy↔Structure/process | Does organizational design permit the intended pattern? | Coordination/incentive mismatch. |
| Strategy↔Environment | Are assumptions/configuration suited to relevant contingencies? | External misfit. |
| Level↔Level | Do subordinate strategies implement, constrain or legitimately challenge higher choices? | Vertical conflict. |
| Present↔Future | Does current optimization preserve required renewal/options? | Temporal incoherence. |

## 3.6 Configuration: the whole can matter

Miller's configuration tradition argues that organizations often appear as cohesive gestalts in which strategy, structure and environment are interrelated. The contribution is not that one universal configuration exists, but that relationships may be reciprocal and system-wide. This supports examining clusters rather than isolated pairwise fits.

However, configuration language can become dangerously holistic: if every successful organization is retrospectively called a coherent gestalt, the concept loses discriminating power. This issue is reserved for Pass 3.

## 3.7 Evolution toward fit

Siggelkow's longitudinal work is especially useful because it treats fit as something that develops. Core elements can be thickened by reinforcing elaborations, new cores can be patched in, elements can coast, and obsolete cores can be trimmed. This makes coherence dynamic rather than a one-time design exercise.

The implication is important: a new element need not fit perfectly on day one if there is a credible path toward a viable configuration. Conversely, an apparently coherent mature system may carry high reconfiguration cost.

## 3.8 Vertical and horizontal coherence

Vertical alignment concerns relations across levels: for example, whether functional/project choices implement or contradict a business/corporate logic. Horizontal coherence concerns relations among choices at roughly the same strategic level or across functions that jointly implement a strategy.

RQ-03 prevents a simplistic hierarchy. A project can have its own strategy while simultaneously serving as an implementation vehicle for a higher-level strategy. Therefore 'aligned' must always name the reference object.

## 3.9 Local rationality can create global incoherence

Rivkin and Siggelkow's work on interdependent decisions shows that delegation, cross-domain interdependencies and differences between local and global incentives can leave organizations at sticking points that are not global optima. This gives a mechanism for a familiar strategic failure: every manager can make locally reasonable choices while the total configuration remains poor.

Thus coherence cannot be inferred from local approval or local KPI success.

## 3.10 Coherence and opportunity selection

RQ-06 now receives an important extension. A project can pass attractiveness, capability and risk tests yet still deserve NOT NOW or NO-GO because its interactions degrade a more valuable strategic configuration. Conversely, an opportunity that initially disrupts coherence may be the seed of a new module or Strategy Review.

Therefore a Go/No-Go decision should ask not only 'does this fit?' but 'what does this change in the system of choices?'

## 3.11 Temporal coherence

March's exploration/exploitation problem makes time central. A configuration optimized for present exploitation can erode future exploration. What appears coherent at a short horizon may be incoherent with long-term renewal. Strategic coherence therefore cannot be assessed without naming the time horizon.

This is the first major warning against maximal fit.

## 3.12 First-pass coherence test

1. Define focal Strategy/object, actor, level and time horizon.

2. List material strategic choices/commitments rather than every organizational activity.

3. Identify strong interdependencies among them.

4. Check direct contradictions and shared-resource conflicts.

5. Check whether important causal mechanisms reinforce, depend on or neutralize one another.

6. Check vertical and horizontal relationships.

7. Identify elements whose value depends on complements.

8. Identify modules that can remain weakly coupled.

9. Check whether present fit closes future strategic options.

10. Ask what evidence would make the current configuration no longer desirable.

## Pass 1 conclusion

Strategic Coherence appears to be a useful system-level construct when it is defined relationally and scoped to a focal actor, level and horizon. Minimum consistency is necessary for deliberate Strategy; reinforcement/complementarity can create additional value, but universal mutual reinforcement is not yet established. Coherence should not be equated with maximum alignment.

# 4. Research Pass 2 — Independent Adversarial Re-run

## Adversarial question

If Pass 1 overvalues coherence, what theories or cases show that inconsistency, separation, loose coupling or competing logics can improve adaptation and long-run performance?

## 4.1 Exploration versus exploitation

March provides a direct challenge. Adaptive processes tend to refine exploitation faster than exploration, creating short-run effectiveness that can become self-destructive over time. A highly aligned system around current success may therefore be strategically dangerous. This weakens any static proposition that more coherence monotonically improves performance.

Revision: coherence quality must include preservation of strategically necessary exploration, not only current-system fit.

## 4.2 Ambidexterity: contradiction can be organized

Tushman & O'Reilly and later ambidexterity research show organizations may need simultaneously different structures, processes or logics for incremental and discontinuous activity. These parts can look inconsistent if judged against one operating template, yet be coherent at a higher level because the separation itself serves the strategic problem.

Revision: local inconsistency can be globally coherent. The reference level is decisive.

## 4.3 Fit creates change costs

Milgrom & Roberts explicitly note that complementarities can make change slow, painful and uncertain because changing one element without complements can reduce performance. Miller's configuration work similarly emphasizes resistance to second-order change. Fit therefore has a shadow price: tightly reinforcing systems can be difficult to reconfigure.

Revision: assess both value of current fit and cost of future reconfiguration.

## 4.4 Sequence can outperform fixed configuration

Siggelkow & Levinthal's work on competency traps suggests sequences of organizational structures can outperform fixed structures even in stable environments. This attacks the idea that the task is to discover one coherent end-state configuration.

Revision: coherence can be staged and temporary; a sequence of partially different configurations may be strategically superior.

## 4.5 Weak coupling and modularity

If interdependencies are limited, forcing integration creates coordination cost without corresponding benefit. A multi-business organization, experimental unit or project portfolio may rationally contain modules with strong internal coherence and weak cross-module fit.

Revision: only material interdependencies justify integration. Coherence is not organizational uniformity.

## 4.6 Coalignment is not one construct

Venkatraman's methodological critique is important: strategic coalignment has been conceptualized as internal consistency and as alignment with external/organizational contingencies, among other forms, with poor theoretical clarification. This directly warns RQ-09 against building one omnibus coherence score.

Revision: preserve relation types instead of collapsing them into a scalar 'coherence level'.

## 4.7 Pass 2 revised proposition

A high-quality Strategy does not maximize fit. It manages material interdependencies: eliminating destructive contradictions, exploiting valuable complementarities, and deliberately preserving separation/tension where integration would reduce exploration, option value or adaptability. This proposition now goes to the destructive third pass.

# 5. Research Pass 3 — Concept Destruction / Lock-in Audit

## Purpose

Pass 3 assumes that both previous passes may have converged on an attractive but empty compromise — 'be coherent, but not too coherent'. It therefore attacks the construct itself rather than merely adding counterarguments.

## 5.1 Locked-position test

Locked position detected: by naming the chapter Strategic Coherence, the research risks treating coherence as inherently desirable and interpreting every successful configuration as evidence for it. To unlock the position, the default question is reversed: why not delete the concept and speak only about specific interdependencies, contradictions and complementarities?

Result: the broad word 'coherence' adds little unless it functions as an umbrella diagnostic that forces system-level relational analysis. It should not be treated as an independent causal variable without specifying underlying relations.

## 5.2 Remove-the-concept test

If the term Strategic Coherence is removed, we can still ask whether choices contradict, complement, compete for resources, require common capabilities, align across levels or create reconfiguration costs. Most analytical work survives. Therefore coherence is not a primitive mechanism. Its defensible role is a higher-order integrative question: 'Have we examined the material relations that make the set of choices function as a strategy rather than independent decisions?'

Surviving claim: coherence is a diagnostic/system property, not a magic explanatory cause.

## 5.3 Tautology / circularity test

Bad formulation: 'A strategy is coherent when its elements fit; fit means the elements are coherent.' This is circular and non-testable. A usable coherence claim must name the relation: contradiction, dependency, complementarity, resource competition, causal interference, hierarchy, temporal trade-off or modular separation.

Revision: prohibit unsupported sentences such as 'these initiatives are strategically coherent' without a specified relational mechanism.

## 5.4 Generic-language test

Phrases such as 'all parts should work together', 'align resources with strategy' and 'ensure strategic fit' have low discriminating power. Almost any manager can agree with them while making opposite decisions.

Revision: the chapter's practical questions must be capable of changing a decision. Example: 'If initiative A scales, which scarce capability required by choice B becomes unavailable?' has decision power; 'Are A and B aligned?' often does not.

## 5.5 Construct redundancy test

| **Candidate synonym** | **Can it replace coherence?** | **Verdict** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consistency | No; detects contradiction but not positive interaction. | Necessary subrelation, not equivalent. |
| Fit | Partly; overloaded term with internal/external/activity meanings. | Too ambiguous alone. |
| Complementarity | No; precise positive interaction, but not all coherence requires complementarity. | Strong subconstruct. |
| Alignment | No; often directional/hierarchical or contingency relation. | Subconstruct/interface. |
| Integration | No; describes degree of joining/coordination, which can be excessive. | Not equivalent. |
| Configuration | No; describes pattern/gestalt, not necessarily quality. | Related representation/theory. |

Result: coherence survives as an umbrella only if its subrelations remain explicit.

## 5.6 Hidden normativity test

Claim 'Strategy should be coherent' is normative. Evidence does not justify maximal integration. The defensible norm is narrower: deliberate Strategy should not contain material, unrecognized contradictions that undermine its own intended logic; known tensions may be legitimate if their strategic purpose and governance are explicit.

This becomes the candidate minimum property for RQ-11, not 'everything reinforces everything'.

## 5.7 Unfalsifiability test

Could any successful organization be called coherent after the fact? Yes, unless the analysis specifies ex ante which relationships should reinforce or conflict and what evidence would contradict the claim. Therefore retrospective narrative coherence is not evidence.

Revision: coherence claims should generate relational expectations. If A is claimed to complement B, there should be a reason to expect A's value/effectiveness to depend on B or vice versa.

## 5.8 Minimal counterexample test

| **Strong thesis attacked** | **Minimal counterexample** | **Outcome** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every strategic choice must reinforce every other. | Two independent geographic businesses under one owner. | Destroyed. |
| More alignment is always better. | Exploration unit forced into core exploitation processes. | Destroyed. |
| Inconsistency means bad Strategy. | Separate exploit/explore structures serving one higher-order objective. | Destroyed. |
| Complementarity is always valuable. | Strong complements that make transition to a new regime prohibitively costly. | Destroyed. |
| Local fit implies global coherence. | Two departments optimize interdependent choices under conflicting incentives. | Destroyed. |
| A coherent narrative proves coherent Strategy. | Consistent presentation with competing resource commitments. | Destroyed. |

## 5.9 Level/object confusion test

A configuration can be incoherent at one level and coherent at another. Structural separation of two business models may violate functional uniformity while serving corporate portfolio logic. Conversely, two functions may appear aligned to a corporate slogan while undermining the business-level mechanism. Every coherence judgment therefore requires focal object, actor/authority, level and horizon.

## 5.10 Over-integration test

The strongest danger in the chapter is turning coherence into a command for integration. Pass 3 rejects this. Integration is justified by material interdependence, not by aesthetics. Where interactions are weak, modularity reduces coordination burden and can protect experimentation. Where interactions are strong, unmanaged separation can create system failure.

## 5.11 Decision-usefulness test

The concept survives only if it changes decisions. It does so in four cases: (1) reveals a hidden contradiction that changes Go/No-Go; (2) identifies a missing complement whose absence changes expected value; (3) shows local optimization harms system performance; (4) shows that forced alignment would destroy useful separation/optionality. Outside such relational consequences, 'coherence' is descriptive decoration.

## 5.12 Strongest surviving claims

- Strategic Coherence is not a single mechanism and should not be treated as a scalar universal performance driver.

- Its useful role is as a scoped system-level diagnostic of material relationships among strategic choices and their implementation conditions.

- For deliberate Strategy, unrecognized material contradictions are a quality defect.

- Positive reinforcement/complementarity can strengthen Strategy but is not required among all elements.

- Local inconsistency, modularity or tension can be globally coherent when deliberately serving a higher-level strategic problem.

- Tight fit can increase both current-system performance and reconfiguration cost; therefore adaptability must be considered.

- Every coherence judgment must specify focal actor/object, level, horizon and relation type.

- A coherence statement without a named relational mechanism has insufficient decision value.

# 6. Post-Destruction Model — Relational Coherence

After three passes, the chapter abandons the idea of a single 'coherence score'. The surviving model is a Relational Coherence Audit.

| **Relation type** | **Diagnostic question** | **Possible valid states** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contradiction | Does one choice materially undermine another's mechanism/outcome? | Remove / redesign / explicitly accept tension. |
| Dependency | Does A require B to work? | Provide complement / stage / reject A. |
| Complementarity | Does A raise the value/effectiveness of B? | Bundle / sequence / protect joint system. |
| Resource competition | Do A and B claim the same scarce capacity? | Prioritize / separate horizon / acquire capacity. |
| Causal interference | Does one mechanism neutralize another? | Redesign / isolate / choose. |
| Vertical relation | Does lower-level Strategy implement or legitimately challenge higher-level choice? | Align / escalate / Strategy Review. |
| Modularity | Can elements operate with limited interdependence? | Keep weakly coupled; avoid forced integration. |
| Temporal relation | Does current fit destroy future options/exploration? | Create slack/options/separation. |
| External contingency | Does configuration depend on environmental condition? | Monitor / redesign / accept conditional fit. |
| Reconfiguration cost | How costly is changing a tightly coupled system? | Preserve flexibility / plan transition. |

# 16. Application Note — separated from theory

In future company strategy work, RQ-09 should prevent a common error: approving several individually attractive projects and assuming they constitute one strategy. The relevant test will be whether they compete for the same scarce capabilities, create shared assets/knowledge, reinforce or neutralize one another, require incompatible operating models, or deliberately remain modular. Current company ideas are not used as evidence for the theory.

# 21. Final Educational Synthesis

RQ-09 began with an intuitive proposition: good Strategy should be coherent. After three passes, that statement survives only in a much narrower and more useful form.

Strategic Coherence is not a universal mechanism, not a synonym for fit, and not a score measuring how neatly everything aligns. Most of the analytical work can be expressed through more precise relations: contradiction, dependency, complementarity, resource competition, causal interference, vertical relation, modularity, temporal trade-off and reconfiguration cost. The term coherence is useful only as a system-level diagnostic asking whether those material relations have been examined together.

For deliberate Strategy, the strongest surviving minimum is not 'all choices reinforce one another'. It is that material contradictions capable of undermining the Strategy's own intended logic should not remain unrecognized. Positive complementarities can make a strategy stronger, but independent modules may coexist. Local inconsistency can be globally rational when separation protects exploration or different business logics. Conversely, calling every conflict 'ambidexterity' would make the concept unfalsifiable.

The chapter also establishes a cost of fit. Tightly reinforcing configurations can generate value, but the same interdependence can increase switching and reconfiguration costs. Therefore the quality of a configuration cannot be assessed only by current internal fit; it must also consider the future option set and ability to adapt.

This creates the direct handoff to RQ-10. RQ-09 asks whether the current system of choices makes sense together. RQ-10 must ask what happens when uncertainty, learning and emergence require that system to change — and how Strategy can adapt without dissolving into opportunism.
