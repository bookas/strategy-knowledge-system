# 3. Part 01 Purpose

Part 01 addresses the foundational question:

> What is Strategy, why does it exist, and what properties turn a set of intentions, decisions, actions, or realized patterns into Strategy?

Part 01 is intended to establish the conceptual foundation for the rest of the Strategy Knowledge Book.

It is system-forming and must therefore be treated as a Core block.

# 4. Completed Research Questions

## RQ-01 — Nature of Strategy

Status:

Completed research draft.

Current working conclusion:

Strategy is treated as a significant organizational logic of direction and choice expressed through interconnected decisions or realized patterns under constraints and uncertainty.

A pedagogical four-layer model was developed:

- Direction;
- Choice;
- Configuration;
- Adaptation.

Important qualification:

This four-layer model is a synthesis for learning and analysis, not a universal ontology of Strategy.

Key distinctions:

Strategy must not be reduced to:

- Plan;
- Goal;
- Position;
- Resource;
- Pattern alone.

The distinction between intended and realized Strategy remains important.

Descriptive and normative uses of the term Strategy must be separated.

Current issue carried forward:

Earlier formulations risked implying that explicit choice is universally necessary for Strategy.

Revalidation narrowed this claim.

## RQ-02 — Function of Strategy

Status:

Completed research draft.

Current working conclusion:

Strategy serves several organizational functions, including:

- focus;
- coordination;
- temporal orientation;
- handling of uncertainty;
- renewal and adaptation.

Working proposition:

> Strategy does not remove uncertainty or constraints; it organizes organizational behavior in the face of them.

Important qualification:

These functions are not treated as a closed universal list.

The model is pedagogical and integrative.

## RQ-03 — Object, Levels & Strategic Agency

Status:

Completed research draft.

Current version:

v1.1 supersedes v1 for active use.

Core distinction:

Level, object, actor, agency, and decision authority must not be collapsed.

Working classification:

Object / Scope
→ Actor / Agency
→ Decision Authority / Dependencies
→ Strategic Decision Space
→ Relation to Other Strategies

Important open issue:

The relationship between strategic agency and emergent Strategy remains only partially resolved.

Revalidation finding:

Strategy formation does not always require a clearly identifiable prior chooser.

Selection, legitimation, articulation, and formation may occur at different times and by different actors.

## RQ-04 — Boundaries of Strategy

Status:

Completed research draft.

Core output:

A relation taxonomy for distinguishing Strategy from adjacent concepts.

Relation types include:

- antecedent/input;
- constraint/boundary;
- component;
- representation;
- implementation vehicle;
- operationalization;
- outcome/state;
- evidence/analysis;
- nested Strategy.

Important distinctions:

- Purpose, Vision, and Mission are related to Strategy but not identical to Strategy.
- Goal is not Strategy.
- Plan and roadmap are not Strategy.
- Initiative and project may implement Strategy.
- Tactics are level-dependent.
- Operational effectiveness is not Strategy.
- Strategic decision is not automatically Strategy.
- Resources and capabilities are not automatically Strategy.
- Strategic position and competitive advantage are not universal synonyms for Strategy.
- Narrative, document, or framework is not Strategy.

Open boundary:

The relationship between Strategy and Business Model is school-dependent and remains intentionally bounded rather than universally fixed.

## RQ-05 — Choice, Scarcity & Trade-offs

Status:

Completed research draft.

Current working conclusion:

“Strategy = choice” is too strong as a universal definition.

For deliberate or formulated Strategy, meaningful choice is a strong candidate requirement.

For realized emergent Strategy, explicit prior choice may not exist.

Important distinctions:

- scarcity is broader than capital scarcity;
- constraint is not identical to choice;
- opportunity cost must be considered broadly;
- Porter-style trade-offs are influential but not universal;
- refusal can be strategically meaningful when it protects a more important commitment;
- commitment can generate capability, credibility, learning, and lock-in.

Optionality conclusion:

Optionality can be strategically meaningful only where there is an identifiable option object, cost, information value, and exercise/abandon condition.

“Keeping all doors open” is not sufficient.

## RQ-06 — Strategic Go / No-Go

Status:

Completed research draft.

Current working conclusion:

Go/No-Go is not a separate Strategy type.

It is better understood as a decision architecture translating Strategy into governed commitments.

Dimensions that should remain distinct include:

- strategic fit;
- value;
- evidence;
- feasibility;
- capability;
- opportunity cost;
- timing;
- downside;
- reversibility;
- learning value.

Working verdict architecture:

- GO;
- CONDITIONAL GO;
- LEARN / OPTION;
- NOT NOW;
- NO-GO;
- STRATEGY REVIEW.

Important qualification:

Go/No-Go is not universal to all forms of Strategy.

It applies most strongly to deliberate commitment and legitimation processes.

## RQ-07 — Diagnosis & Starting Point

Status:

Completed research draft.

Current working conclusion:

Strategic diagnosis is a selective, evidence-informed, revisable representation of a situation constructed to make consequential strategic search and choice possible.

Important distinctions include:

- observation / data;
- signal;
- interpretation;
- issue;
- symptom;
- problem;
- opportunity;
- constraint;
- cause hypothesis;
- diagnosis;
- unknown.

Important qualification:

Diagnosis should not be treated as universally first in chronological time.

An organization may act first and learn through action.

The stronger proposition is:

Diagnosis is logically required for defending deliberate strategic choice, even if the diagnosis itself is partly produced through action.

## RQ-08 — Causal Logic

Status:

Completed research draft.

Current working conclusion:

Deliberate Strategy contains at least implicit beliefs about how action is expected to produce outcomes.

For deliberate or formulated Strategy, explicit causal logic is a strong quality requirement.

It is not treated as a universal necessary property of realized emergent Strategy.

Working architecture:

Strategic Argument
+
Epistemic Envelope

Important conclusions:

- causal maps expose assumptions but do not prove causation;
- causal ambiguity is normal;
- attempted disconfirmation is more realistic than strict falsification in open organizational systems;
- effectuation provides an important boundary case.

## RQ-09 — Strategic Coherence & Alignment

Status:

Completed research draft.

Current working conclusion:

Strategic Coherence is not treated as a primitive causal mechanism.

It is a system-level diagnostic umbrella.

Important minimum proposition:

For deliberate Strategy, material unrecognized contradictions capable of undermining intended logic are a strategic defect.

Known and managed tensions may be legitimate.

Every coherence judgment should identify:

- focal object;
- actor;
- level;
- time horizon.

Current audit architecture includes relation types such as:

- contradiction;
- dependency;
- complementarity;
- resource competition;
- causal interference;
- vertical relation;
- modularity;
- temporal relation;
- external contingency;
- reconfiguration cost.

Open conceptual debt:

Potential overlap with Systems Thinking and configuration logic must be revisited later.

## RQ-10 — Uncertainty, Emergence & Adaptation

Status:

Completed research draft.

Current working conclusion:

Uncertainty is heterogeneous and should not be treated as one generic category.

Deliberate and emergent Strategy are better understood as endpoints or modes rather than mutually exclusive categories.

Important conclusions:

- emergence is descriptive before it is normative;
- learning may improve Strategy or reinforce traps;
- commitment and adaptation are not opposites;
- real-options reasoning is bounded;
- effectuation is an important boundary case;
- dynamic capabilities must be protected from tautological interpretation;
- adaptation is an umbrella for strategically consequential updating, not a primitive mechanism.

Important architecture finding:

The Knowledge Book currently privileges deliberate strategic reasoning.

This is useful for criticizability but should not be mistaken for the only route by which Strategy forms.

Working update logic:

Signal
→ Assumption
→ Causal / Diagnostic Model
→ Initiative / Resource
→ Choice / Boundary
→ Strategy Review

Rejected universal formulation:

“Stable core + adaptive periphery” is not accepted as a universal architecture of Strategy.

## RQ-11 — Strategy Quality & Failure

Status:

Completed research draft.

Current working conclusion:

Strategy quality is multi-object.

Possible quality objects include:

- decision/process;
- strategic content;
- implementation;
- adaptation;
- outcome;
- evaluation.

Important proposition:

Outcome is evidence about Strategy but is not identical to Strategy quality.

Candidate minimum requirements for deliberate/formulated Strategy include:

- sufficient representation of the strategic problem or opportunity;
- consequential choice or commitment architecture;
- defensible means–ends logic;
- absence of unrecognized material contradiction;
- sufficient feasibility;
- appropriate epistemic posture;
- connection to action and governance.

These are not sufficient conditions for success.

Failure should be localized before the whole Strategy is declared a failure.

Important boundary:

Explicit prior articulation cannot be required for emergent Strategy.
