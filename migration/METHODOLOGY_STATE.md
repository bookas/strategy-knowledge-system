# Strategy Knowledge System — Methodology State

## 1. Document Purpose

This document records the current research, review, validation, and knowledge-governance methodology of the Strategy Knowledge System at the time of migration to Git and Codex.

Its purpose is preservation.

It captures the methodology that has already been developed and accepted during the Strategy Knowledge Book project before that methodology is decomposed into canonical files under `methodology/` and role-specific instructions under `agents/`.

This document is not:

- a research chapter;
- a repository instruction file;
- a final Codex agent specification;
- a project status report;
- a replacement for detailed future methodology modules.

During migration, it serves as the authoritative snapshot of the methodology that must be preserved.

---

# 2. Methodological Objective

The methodology is designed to produce strategic knowledge that is:

- evidence-informed;
- conceptually precise;
- causally defensible;
- adversarially tested;
- explicit about boundaries;
- resistant to confirmation bias;
- cross-chapter consistent;
- traceable;
- revisable;
- useful for later strategic decisions.

The methodology is not designed to maximize document length, number of citations, number of frameworks, or apparent certainty.

Its purpose is to improve the quality of knowledge.

---

# 3. Core Epistemic Principle

The system follows the general logic:

Research
→ Challenge
→ Comparison
→ Boundary Testing
→ System Review
→ Adjudication
→ Controlled Knowledge Change

Research agents may propose knowledge.

Review agents challenge knowledge.

Review findings do not automatically modify established knowledge.

Significant knowledge changes require adjudication and controlled revision.

---

# 4. Universal Research Chapter Protocol

Every major research chapter follows a defined lifecycle.

Current full protocol:

Pre-Chapter Overview
→ Human Annotation
→ AI Research & Review Annotation
→ Layer 0 / Chapter Passport
→ Initial Filling / Starting Position
→ Main Research Questions
→ Reference Reconnaissance
→ Research Pass 1
→ Independent Adversarial Research Pass 2
→ Research Pass 3 for Core/System-Forming Chapters
→ Audit / Meta Layer
→ Cumulative Self-Review
→ Claim Ledger
→ Chapter Exit Gate
→ Final Educational Synthesis
→ Reading
→ Open Questions / Handoffs

This protocol cannot be shortened for Core/system-forming work without an explicit decision.

---

# 5. Pre-Chapter Overview

Before research begins for every new major chapter, a Pre-Chapter Overview must be produced and discussed.

Its purpose is to prevent premature research before the conceptual role of the chapter is understood.

Required elements:

1. Chapter title and place in the architecture.
2. What the chapter is about in plain language.
3. Why the chapter is needed.
4. What conceptual gap it addresses.
5. Connection backward to previous knowledge.
6. Connection forward to later knowledge.
7. Main research questions.
8. One to three competing hypotheses or controversial propositions.
9. Why the chapter matters to the overall Knowledge System.
10. Intended depth where relevant.

Competing hypotheses must not be presented as already proven conclusions.

Research begins only after the chapter framing is sufficiently accepted.

---

# 6. Human Annotation

Each major chapter begins with a short Human Annotation.

Its purpose is educational orientation.

It should explain:

- why the chapter exists;
- what the learner should gain;
- why the issue matters;
- how it connects to strategic reasoning.

The Human Annotation should not become a substitute for research.

---

# 7. AI Research & Review Annotation

Each major chapter also includes an AI-facing research specification.

It should define:

- research task;
- scope;
- boundaries;
- incoming findings from previous chapters;
- claims that cannot be assumed;
- competing hypotheses;
- required handoffs;
- contrary-evidence requirement;
- cumulative-review requirement;
- relevant conceptual debts;
- dependencies on prior research.

The purpose is to prevent the research process from silently treating previous working conclusions as established truth.

---

# 8. Layer 0 / Chapter Passport

Layer 0 defines the chapter before substantive research begins.

Current expected fields include:

- place in Knowledge Book architecture;
- chapter purpose;
- conceptual gap;
- central research question;
- what the chapter contains;
- what it excludes;
- learning outcome;
- prerequisites;
- dependencies;
- concepts it leads to;
- interfaces with other chapters;
- overlap risks;
- research-question summary;
- application relevance;
- open interfaces;
- AI audit specification.

Layer 0 is a research-control artifact.

It is not intended to predetermine the chapter conclusion.

---

# 9. Initial Filling / Starting Position

Before deep research, the chapter may record a starting position.

The starting position may contain:

- preliminary understanding;
- inherited claims;
- assumptions;
- terminology;
- known controversies;
- unresolved distinctions.

This is not established knowledge.

Its purpose is to make prior assumptions visible so that research can challenge them.

---

# 10. Research Questions

Research is organized around questions rather than around accumulating information.

A research question should identify a real conceptual, causal, boundary, evaluative, or decision-relevant uncertainty.

The number of research questions is not fixed.

Research coherence is more important than question quantity.

---

# 11. Research Question Entry Protocol

The first research question in a chapter follows approximately:

Layer 0
→ Research Question
→ Schools / Theory
→ Research

For RQ-02 and later questions, the entry protocol expands to:

Layer 0
→ Previous Research Context
→ Schools / Lenses
→ Previous Findings
→ Why Current Working Lens
→ What Is Still Unknown
→ Current Research Question
→ Competing Hypotheses
→ Research

This prevents later research questions from becoming disconnected essays.

---

# 12. Reference Reconnaissance

Before deep synthesis, perform Reference Reconnaissance.

Purpose:

- identify major schools;
- locate primary works;
- identify important competing traditions;
- identify relevant empirical research;
- detect major terminology differences;
- find potential counterevidence;
- determine where secondary sources may be distorting primary theory.

Reference Reconnaissance is not yet the final research synthesis.

---

# 13. Source Hierarchy

Current preferred source hierarchy:

1. Primary academic works and original concept authors.
2. Peer-reviewed academic research.
3. Universities and established research institutions.
4. Standards and recognized international organizations.
5. High-quality professional practice and documented cases.
6. Consulting and practitioner material as supplementary evidence.

Source quality takes priority over source language.

No category is automatically correct.

Primary authors must also be critically evaluated.

---

# 14. Research Pass 1 — Constructive Research

Research Pass 1 is the primary knowledge-building pass.

Its purpose is to construct the strongest defensible understanding of the research question.

It should examine:

- definitions;
- major schools;
- competing interpretations;
- mechanisms;
- empirical support;
- distinctions;
- boundary conditions;
- consequences;
- strategic usefulness.

For substantial conclusions, the preferred reasoning structure is:

Research Question
→ Answer
→ Reasons / Evidence For
→ Reasons / Evidence Against
→ What Influenced the Conclusion
→ Final Conclusion

Where relevant, include what would cause the conclusion to be reconsidered.

---

# 15. Research Pass 2 — Independent Adversarial Pass

Research Pass 2 is mandatory for substantial research chapters.

It must not merely edit or polish Pass 1.

Its starting question is:

> If the conclusion of Pass 1 is wrong, what is the strongest theory, model, evidence, alternative explanation, or boundary case that would show it?

Pass 2 should actively search for:

- strongest counter-models;
- contrary evidence;
- alternative explanations;
- domain limitations;
- boundary cases;
- level-of-analysis problems;
- causal alternatives;
- omitted schools;
- contradictory evidence;
- false universality;
- misleading terminology.

Pass 2 should be meaningfully independent from Pass 1.

Its purpose is attempted disconfirmation, not rhetorical balance.

---

# 16. Second-Pass Independence Test

Pass 2 should be evaluated for independence.

Questions include:

- Did it introduce genuinely new counterarguments?
- Did it search outside the conceptual frame selected by Pass 1?
- Did it identify credible alternative schools?
- Did it test boundary cases?
- Did it challenge causal mechanisms?
- Did it question whether the original research question itself was framed correctly?
- Did it merely rephrase Pass 1?

If Pass 2 only restates or mildly qualifies Pass 1, it has failed the independence test.

---

# 17. Research Pass 3 — Concept Destruction / Lock-in Audit

Research Pass 3 is mandatory for Core or system-forming chapters.

Its purpose is not merely to find contrary evidence.

It attacks the surviving conceptual construction itself.

Pass 3 should test for:

- locked positions;
- circularity;
- tautologies;
- generic management language;
- hidden normativity;
- construct redundancy;
- level confusion;
- object confusion;
- unfalsifiability;
- over-integration;
- rational boundary cases;
- minimal counterexamples;
- unnecessary universalization;
- architecture lock-in.

Mandatory attack questions include:

### Remove-the-Concept Test

If this concept were removed, what explanatory or decision capability would actually be lost?

If little is lost, the construct may be redundant.

### Minimal Counterexample Test

What is the smallest plausible case that violates the proposed claim?

### Decision Usefulness Test

Does the concept improve a real distinction, explanation, prediction, diagnosis, or decision?

Or does it merely rename something already known?

### Strongest Surviving Claim

After destruction attempts, what is the strongest claim that still survives?

Pass 3 should prefer a narrower defensible claim over a broad fragile claim.

---

# 18. Definition of Core / System-Forming Chapter

A chapter should receive Core/system-forming treatment when its conclusions materially determine:

- definitions used throughout the book;
- the architecture of later chapters;
- foundational distinctions;
- research methodology;
- evaluation logic;
- organizational translation;
- major cross-chapter dependencies.

Core status implies higher review depth.

It does not mean the chapter's claims are immune to later revision.

---

# 19. Causal Claim Audit

Claims containing causal logic require additional examination.

The audit should ask:

- What is the proposed cause?
- What is the proposed effect?
- What mechanism connects them?
- What assumptions are required?
- What alternative mechanisms could produce the same observation?
- Is evidence causal or merely correlational?
- At what level does the mechanism operate?
- Under what conditions should it fail?
- What evidence would weaken the claim?

Plausibility is not sufficient evidence of causation.

---

# 20. Transfer Validity Test

Knowledge derived from one domain, context, organizational type, market, or level must not automatically be transferred to another.

The test should ask:

- Where was the original claim developed?
- What was its object?
- What was its level of analysis?
- What institutional conditions existed?
- What environmental conditions existed?
- Which assumptions are context-dependent?
- What changes in the target context?
- Which parts can reasonably transfer?
- Which parts require new evidence?

Transfer should be justified rather than assumed.

---

# 21. Contradiction Matrix

The Contradiction Matrix records potentially incompatible claims across the Knowledge System.

It should distinguish:

- true contradiction;
- apparent contradiction caused by different definitions;
- different levels of analysis;
- different objects;
- different time horizons;
- different formation modes;
- different boundary conditions;
- unresolved theoretical disagreement.

Contradictions should not be resolved through wording alone.

---

# 22. Concept Boundary Registry

The Concept Boundary Registry records distinctions between neighboring concepts.

Its purpose is to prevent conceptual collapse.

Examples of relations that may need tracking:

- Strategy vs Goal;
- Strategy vs Plan;
- Strategy vs Business Model;
- Strategy vs Initiative;
- Strategy vs Tactics;
- Strategy vs Operational Effectiveness;
- Strategy vs Capability;
- Strategy vs Position;
- Strategy vs Narrative.

Boundary status may be:

- relatively stable;
- school-dependent;
- context-dependent;
- unresolved;
- intentionally overlapping.

---

# 23. Conceptual Debt

Not every conceptual problem must be solved immediately.

The system allows Conceptual Debt.

Current useful classification:

### Safe Borrowing

A concept is used provisionally with sufficiently clear boundaries and low downstream risk.

### Controlled Debt

A known unresolved issue exists but does not currently block research.

It must be registered and revisited.

### Blocking Debt

The unresolved concept is sufficiently important that proceeding would risk contaminating downstream knowledge.

Research should stop or narrow until the debt is addressed.

Conceptual Debt must not become a mechanism for indefinitely ignoring problems.

---

# 24. Base Review Block

Between research questions or major research stages, a review block may record:

1. Potential Issues
2. Possible Conceptual Gaps
3. Cross-Chapter Concept Use
4. Possible Duplication / Productive Repetition
5. Deferred Decisions

The review block is a meta-layer.

Its findings do not automatically rewrite the educational chapter.

---

# 25. Productive Repetition vs Harmful Duplication

Concept recurrence across chapters is not automatically a defect.

The review should distinguish:

### Productive Repetition

The same concept is legitimately used for a different question, mechanism, level, or learning purpose.

### Working Use

A concept is temporarily borrowed before its dedicated chapter.

### Harmful Duplication

The same construct is independently rebuilt with incompatible meanings or unnecessary redundancy.

The goal is conceptual coherence, not elimination of all repetition.

---

# 26. Cumulative Self-Review

Every substantial new research block must be reviewed against the accumulated Knowledge System.

Cumulative review should examine:

- relevant prior chapters;
- prior review blocks;
- open findings;
- conceptual debts;
- watch items;
- deferred decisions;
- previous review conclusions;
- downstream dependencies.

Findings may be classified as:

- open;
- strengthened;
- weakened;
- resolved;
- superseded;
- deferred.

The purpose is to prevent local research improvements from producing global inconsistency.

---

# 27. Claim Ledger

Important claims should progressively be represented in a structured Claim Ledger.

A claim record may include:

- Claim ID;
- Claim;
- Scope;
- Status;
- Evidence;
- Counterevidence;
- Boundary Conditions;
- Formation Mode where relevant;
- Object / Level where relevant;
- Dependencies;
- Downstream Impact;
- Review History.

The Claim Ledger is intended to make knowledge traceable and reviewable.

The prose chapter remains the educational layer.

---

# 28. No Inline Epistemic Tags

The educational text must not use intrusive inline status labels such as:

- [Established]
- [Contested]
- [Working]
- [Tentative]

Epistemic status should instead be communicated through:

- appropriate wording;
- attribution;
- evidence;
- alternatives;
- boundary conditions;
- citations;
- Claim Ledger;
- review artifacts.

This preserves readability while maintaining epistemic control in the underlying system.

---

# 29. Chapter Exit Gate

A chapter is not complete merely because research text exists.

Before exit, evaluate whether:

- central research questions were answered sufficiently;
- important competing theories were considered;
- counterevidence was examined;
- boundary conditions are visible;
- causal claims were audited where required;
- important contradictions were registered;
- conceptual debt is explicit;
- cumulative review was completed;
- important claims are traceable;
- open questions are recorded;
- downstream handoffs are identified;
- educational synthesis is supported by the research.

For Core chapters, required adversarial depth must also be completed.

---

# 30. Final Educational Synthesis

After research and review, a clean educational synthesis is produced.

The educational text should not reproduce every internal audit operation.

Preferred structure where appropriate:

Concept
→ Why
→ Distinction
→ Causal Mechanism
→ Strategic Question
→ Quality Criteria
→ Antipatterns
→ Approaches to Answering
→ Application Note
→ New Questions

This is a preferred learning architecture, not an immutable template.

---

# 31. Learning Outcome

Each major chapter should define what the learner should be able to understand, distinguish, explain, criticize, or apply after completing it.

Learning Outcome should go beyond:

“know the definition.”

It should emphasize strategic reasoning capability.

---

# 32. Boundary Cases

Boundary Cases are mandatory where they materially test the concept.

They help determine:

- whether the definition is too broad;
- whether it is too narrow;
- whether a rule is falsely universal;
- whether different strategic modes require different criteria.

Boundary cases are especially important for foundational Strategy concepts.

---

# 33. Antipatterns and Failure

Each major block should examine common misuse and failure.

The purpose is not anecdotal entertainment.

For each important failure pattern, ask:

- What failed?
- Why?
- What mechanism produced the failure?
- What consequences followed?
- Which assumption was wrong?
- Was the problem Strategy, implementation, environment, governance, or evaluation?

Failure evidence should inform theory rather than merely illustrate it.

---

# 34. Quality Criteria for Future Answers

The Knowledge System should progressively define criteria for evaluating organizational answers.

Where useful, answer quality may be distinguished as:

- weak;
- acceptable;
- strong;
- overly abstract / overloaded.

The system should explain why an answer has a particular quality level.

This is particularly important for concepts later used directly in organizational strategy formulation.

---

# 35. Framework Evaluation

Frameworks should be evaluated by the question they solve.

For each important framework, ask:

- What problem is it designed to address?
- What assumptions does it make?
- What inputs does it require?
- What does it reveal?
- What does it omit?
- Where is it useful?
- Where can it mislead?
- What decision can it legitimately support?

Completing a framework is not equivalent to creating Strategy.

---

# 36. Application Notes

Company or organization-specific relevance may be included only as a clearly separated application layer.

Application Notes should generally remain a minority of the theoretical chapter.

A working guideline is approximately 10–20% where application is useful.

Application examples must not be used to validate the theory that generated them.

The preferred flow is:

Universal Knowledge
→ Organizational Evidence
→ Candidate Interpretation
→ Strategic Implication

not:

Existing Organizational Idea
→ Supporting Theory

---

# 37. Organizational Translation

The future Application / Translation methodology should protect against confirmation bias.

Organizational translation should require:

- organizational facts;
- evidence;
- explicit assumptions;
- alternatives;
- uncertainty;
- disconfirming evidence;
- strategic consequence;
- review triggers.

Theory provides lenses and questions.

It does not provide company-specific answers automatically.

---

# 38. Retrospective Revalidation

After a major book part is completed, the accumulated system must be revalidated.

Current mandatory structure:

## Pass 1 — Claim-by-Claim Revalidation

Review important claims individually.

## Pass 2 — Cross-System Contradiction & Boundary Review

Test relationships between claims and chapters.

## Pass 3 — Architecture Destruction / Lock-in Audit

Attack the structure that survived local research.

## Pass 4 — Open AI Critique / Unknown-Unknown Red Team

Allow broader critique not constrained by the existing taxonomy of known issues.

The purpose is to identify errors that local chapter review may systematically miss.

---

# 39. Change Register

Retrospective revalidation must not silently rewrite prior knowledge.

Important proposed changes follow:

Finding
→ Original Claim
→ New Evidence / Reasoning
→ Verdict
→ Required Change
→ Downstream Impact
→ Approval
→ Applied Revision

The Change Register preserves intellectual provenance.

---

# 40. Architecture Review

After retrospective revalidation, perform a Knowledge Architecture Review.

Questions include:

- Does the chapter order imply unsupported causal order?
- Are concepts duplicated?
- Are important concepts missing?
- Are system boundaries clear?
- Does one theoretical tradition dominate architecture unintentionally?
- Are research order, learning order, and reality order being confused?
- Are some chapters actually subcomponents of others?
- Are translation layers being confused with theory?
- Is the system becoming unnecessarily complex?
- What architecture would a competing paradigm produce?

Architecture Review may propose changes.

It does not automatically authorize them.

---

# 41. Research Order Is Not Reality Order

A fundamental methodological safeguard is:

> The order in which knowledge is researched or taught must not automatically be interpreted as the order in which organizational reality occurs.

For example:

Diagnosis may be logically necessary to defend deliberate choice while still being produced partly through prior action.

Strategy may emerge before explicit articulation.

Learning may modify earlier strategic choices.

The Knowledge System must not turn its own workflow into an unsupported universal process theory.

---

Research depth depends on the significance of the chapter.

Ordinary substantial chapter:

+
Independent Pass 2
+

+

+
exit gate

Core/system-forming chapter:

Pass 1
+
Independent Pass 2
+
Pass 3 Concept Destruction
+
full relevant audits
+
cumulative review
+

+
exit gate

Major completed book part:

Retrospective Revalidation
+
Architecture Review

Depth is determined by downstream epistemic risk rather than chapter length.

---

# 43. Research Stopping Rule

There is no fixed required number of sources or research questions.

Research may stop when:

- major credible schools are represented;
- important counterarguments are understood;
- additional evidence is no longer materially changing the conclusion;
- boundary conditions are sufficiently understood;
- remaining uncertainty is explicitly recorded;
- the conclusion is sufficient for the intended knowledge purpose.

More research is not automatically better research.

---

# 44. Literature Recommendations

Recommended literature should follow the research question.

For major blocks, the educational layer may recommend approximately one to three important books or equivalent works.

Where useful, specify:

- why the work matters;
- relevant chapters;
- whether it is mandatory, desirable, or deeper reading;
- audiobook suitability where relevant.

Literature recommendations should not drive the research architecture.

---

# 45. Open Questions Register

Open Questions are mandatory.

A question may remain open because:

- evidence is insufficient;
- schools genuinely disagree;
- the issue belongs to a later chapter;
- a boundary is unresolved;
- organizational evidence is required;
- the issue is intentionally deferred.

Open Questions must not be silently converted into conclusions.

# 48. Strategic Knowledge and Organizational Memory

The methodology distinguishes:

Founder or manager knows
≠
Organization possesses knowledge.

Organizational knowledge should be:

- explicit where appropriate;

- reviewable;
- transferable;

# 49. AI Role Separation

The planned AI research architecture separates functions.

Potential roles:

## Researcher

Constructs strongest evidence-based answer.

## Validator

Tests whether claims are adequately supported.

## Falsifier

Attempts to break conclusions.

## Evidence Auditor

Checks evidence quality, provenance, citation support, and overclaiming.

## Architecture Red Team

Attacks system structure and hidden lock-in.

## Alternative Paradigm Reviewer

Attempts to reconstruct the issue through a materially different theoretical lens.

## Adjudicator

Evaluates competing findings and determines what changes should be proposed for approval.

## Orchestrator

Coordinates workflow without becoming the epistemic authority.

Role specifications will later be implemented under `agents/`.

# 50. Research and Review Separation

A central governance principle is:

> Research agents propose knowledge. Review agents challenge knowledge. Only adjudication authorizes knowledge change.

Review findings must not directly overwrite canonical knowledge.

This separation is intended to reduce:

- confirmation bias;
- self-review weakness;
- premature convergence;
- silent knowledge drift.

External model adversarial validation may be used for particularly important Core/system-forming work.

Current candidate architecture:

Primary Research
→ Internal Independent Review
→ Adjudication
→ External Model Gate
→ Cross-Model Adjudication
→ Approved Revision

External model review is not required for every research operation.

Its value is greatest where architectural or foundational lock-in risk is high.

# 63. Current Governing Methodological Principle

The current methodology can be compressed into:

> Build the strongest explanation available, actively try to destroy it, preserve what survives, expose what remains uncertain, test it against the rest of the knowledge system, and change established knowledge only through traceable review.
