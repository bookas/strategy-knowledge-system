# Strategy Knowledge System — Project Context

## 1. Document Purpose

This document defines the stable project-level context for the Strategy Knowledge System.

It explains:

- what the project is;
- why it exists;
- what problem it is intended to solve;
- what kinds of knowledge it contains;
- how research, learning, application, and publication relate to each other;
- what fundamental principles govern the system;
- what the system is not intended to become.

This document is not:

- a research chapter;
- a summary of Strategy theory;
- a migration log;
- a project status report;
- a list of current research findings;
- a substitute for the research methodology;
- a repository instruction file.

Current project state belongs in `PROJECT_STATE.md`.

Migration procedures belong in `MIGRATION_PLAN.md`.

Research methodology belongs initially in `METHODOLOGY_STATE.md` and subsequently in the canonical `methodology/` layer.

Repository and agent behavior belongs in `AGENTS.md` and the relevant files under `agents/`.


# 2. Project Identity

Project name:

**Strategy Knowledge System**

Primary knowledge product:

**Strategy Knowledge Book**

The project is a long-term research, learning, and knowledge-engineering system for developing a rigorous understanding of Strategy and related organizational concepts.

The objective is not merely to produce a book.

The objective is to create a structured body of knowledge that can support:

1. deep learning;
2. strategic reasoning;
3. organizational strategy formulation;
4. critical examination of strategic claims;
5. future organizational application;
6. systematic revision as knowledge develops.


# 3. Why the Project Exists

Strategy is often learned and practiced through fragmented frameworks, terminology, consulting models, simplified definitions, and isolated examples.

This creates several recurring problems:

- concepts with different meanings are treated as synonyms;
- frameworks are used without understanding the question they solve;
- prescriptions are presented as universal principles;
- strategic claims are accepted without examining their causal logic;
- company-specific assumptions contaminate supposedly general theory;
- goals, plans, initiatives, business models, tactics, and Strategy are easily conflated;
- successful outcomes are retrospectively treated as proof of strategic quality;
- failure cases and boundary conditions receive insufficient attention;
- knowledge remains in the founder's or manager's head rather than becoming organizational knowledge.

The Strategy Knowledge System is intended to reduce these problems by building knowledge through explicit research questions, competing explanations, causal reasoning, adversarial review, boundary testing, and controlled revision.


# 4. Primary Purpose

The primary purpose of the project is to build sufficient theoretical and methodological understanding to formulate, evaluate, criticize, govern, and revise organizational Strategy responsibly.

The system should enable a learner or organization to move from:

fragmented strategic vocabulary

to:

structured strategic reasoning

and eventually to:

defensible organizational Strategy.

The Knowledge System therefore precedes company-specific strategy formulation.

It is intended to improve the quality of the questions, distinctions, evidence, reasoning, and decisions used during later strategy work.


# 5. Core Design Principle

The project follows the principle:

> Universal knowledge first. Organizational application second.

Theory must not be constructed to validate an existing organizational idea.

Existing company hypotheses, projects, preferences, or strategic intentions must not be used as evidence that a theoretical proposition is correct.

The intended direction is:

Universal Knowledge
→ Organizational Evidence
→ Candidate Interpretation
→ Strategic Implication
→ Decision

not:

Existing Idea
→ Search for Supporting Theory
→ Retrospective Justification


# 6. Knowledge Architecture

The system separates several different knowledge functions.

## 6.1 Research Corpus

The Research Corpus contains the deepest research layer.

It may be substantially larger and more technical than the material intended for ordinary reading.

Its purpose is to preserve:

- research questions;
- competing theories;
- evidence;
- counterevidence;
- causal arguments;
- boundary conditions;
- conceptual disputes;
- review findings;
- unresolved questions;
- provenance;
- research decisions.

Research depth and reading depth are intentionally different.


## 6.2 Strategy Knowledge Book

The Strategy Knowledge Book is the primary educational synthesis.

It should contain mature knowledge derived from the Research Corpus.

Its purpose is understanding rather than exhaustive documentation of every research operation.

The educational text should remain readable while preserving intellectual rigor.


## 6.3 Curriculum

The Curriculum defines the learning sequence.

Research order, book order, learning order, and the order in which Strategy occurs in organizational reality are not assumed to be identical.

The Curriculum may therefore sequence concepts differently from:

- the historical order of research;
- the causal order of strategic processes;
- the final book architecture.


## 6.4 Structured Knowledge Layer

Important knowledge should not exist only inside prose.

Where useful, the system should maintain structured artifacts such as:

- Claim Ledger;
- Change Register;
- Contradiction Matrix;
- Concept Boundary Registry;
- Conceptual Debt Register;
- Open Questions Register;
- source and evidence records;
- research and review metadata.

This layer enables machine-assisted review, traceability, cross-chapter analysis, and controlled revision.


## 6.5 Application Layer

Organizational application is maintained separately from universal theory.

Application material may include:

- organizational evidence;
- strategic questions;
- candidate interpretations;
- hypotheses;
- possible implications;
- later strategy-development work.

Application Notes must not silently become evidence for universal theoretical claims.


## 6.6 Publication Layer

DOCX, PDF, and other presentation formats may be generated for reading, distribution, or archival purposes.

Publication artifacts are not automatically canonical research sources.

Canonical status must be explicitly defined by repository policy.


# 7. Knowledge Is a System, Not a Collection of Chapters

The project treats strategic knowledge as an interconnected conceptual system.

A chapter cannot be evaluated only internally.

A claim introduced in one chapter may:

- contradict another chapter;
- depend on another concept;
- change meaning at another level of analysis;
- create hidden assumptions downstream;
- duplicate another construct;
- require later revision;
- become invalid under different boundary conditions.

For this reason, cross-chapter review is a core feature of the project.

Local chapter quality is necessary but not sufficient for system quality.


# 8. Research Philosophy

The project is not intended to identify a single authoritative school of Strategy and reproduce it.

It should compare competing traditions and examine where their claims apply.

Research should distinguish, where relevant:

- descriptive from normative claims;
- deliberate from emergent Strategy;
- ex-ante reasoning from ex-post evaluation;
- concept from representation of the concept;
- mechanism from outcome;
- necessary from sufficient conditions;
- empirical evidence from theoretical assertion;
- organizational level from strategic object;
- correlation from causal explanation;
- universal claims from bounded claims.

Disagreement between credible theories is treated as research material rather than noise to be eliminated prematurely.


# 9. Causal Depth

A recurring question throughout the project is:

> Why?

Knowing that a framework, principle, or strategic practice is recommended is insufficient.

The Knowledge System should attempt to determine:

- why it should work;
- through what mechanism;
- under what conditions;
- for what object or level;
- what assumptions it requires;
- what alternative mechanism could explain the same observation;
- what happens when the mechanism fails;
- what evidence would weaken the claim.

Causal explanations must not be treated as proven merely because they are plausible.


# 10. Adversarial Knowledge Building

Research is expected to challenge its own conclusions.

The system therefore separates knowledge construction from adversarial review.

A research conclusion should be exposed, where appropriate, to:

- alternative explanations;
- contrary evidence;
- competing schools;
- boundary cases;
- minimal counterexamples;
- construct redundancy tests;
- circularity tests;
- hidden normative assumptions;
- level-of-analysis errors;
- transfer-validity problems;
- decision-usefulness tests.

For system-forming concepts, surviving an initial research pass is not sufficient for acceptance.


# 11. Failure as Evidence

The project intentionally studies strategic failure and conceptual misuse.

For important concepts, research should ask:

- what failed;
- why it failed;
- whether the theory predicted the failure;
- what assumptions were violated;
- whether failure came from Strategy, implementation, environment, evaluation, or another object;
- what consequences followed.

Success cases alone are insufficient evidence for strategic quality.


# 12. Boundary Conditions

The project rejects unnecessary universalization.

Whenever a strong proposition is made, research should attempt to identify:

- the domain in which it applies;
- the organizational level;
- the strategic object;
- the relevant actor or agency;
- the time horizon;
- formation mode;
- environmental conditions;
- information conditions;
- known exceptions.

A bounded strong claim is preferable to an unsupported universal claim.


# 13. Knowledge Quality

Knowledge quality is not determined by the quantity of sources or the length of a chapter.

Quality depends on factors including:

- conceptual clarity;
- source quality;
- appropriate attribution;
- causal defensibility;
- contrary evidence;
- distinction between competing interpretations;
- explicit boundary conditions;
- cross-system consistency;
- treatment of uncertainty;
- decision usefulness;
- traceability;
- ability to survive adversarial review.

Research should stop expanding when additional material no longer meaningfully improves understanding or changes the decision-relevant conclusion.


# 14. Source Philosophy

Source quality takes priority over source language or convenience.

The preferred hierarchy is generally:

1. primary academic works and original concept authors;
2. peer-reviewed research;
3. universities and established research institutions;
4. recognized standards and international organizations;
5. high-quality professional practice and documented cases;
6. consulting and practitioner material as supplementary evidence.

No source category is automatically correct.

Authority does not remove the requirement for critical evaluation.


# 15. Explicit Uncertainty

The Knowledge System does not require every research question to end with certainty.

Valid outputs may include:

- supported conclusion;
- bounded conclusion;
- competing explanations;
- unresolved disagreement;
- insufficient evidence;
- open question;
- conceptual debt;
- deferred decision.

Uncertainty should be represented rather than hidden through artificial synthesis.


# 16. Organizational Knowledge

The project distinguishes between:

> a person knowing something

and:

> an organization possessing usable knowledge.

Knowledge becomes organizationally useful when it can be:

- accessed;
- understood;
- criticized;
- traced;
- reused;
- updated;
- transferred;
- connected to decisions.

The repository is therefore intended to become part of the organizational memory architecture rather than merely document storage.


# 17. Strategy-to-Application Boundary

The Knowledge System should eventually support organizational strategy formulation.

However, theoretical research and organizational strategy development remain distinct activities.

The application process should require organizational evidence rather than substituting theory for evidence.

A theoretically attractive idea is not automatically appropriate for a particular organization.

Likewise, an organizationally successful practice does not automatically establish a universal theoretical principle.


# 18. Human and AI Roles

AI is used as a research, critique, synthesis, review, and knowledge-management instrument.

AI is not treated as an epistemic authority.

Different AI roles may be deliberately separated, including:

- Researcher;
- Validator;
- Falsifier;
- Evidence Auditor;
- Architecture Red Team;
- Alternative Paradigm Reviewer;
- Adjudicator;
- Orchestrator.

Role separation exists to reduce self-confirmation and premature convergence.

Research agents may propose changes.

Review agents may challenge them.

Knowledge modification should occur through controlled adjudication rather than automatic acceptance of reviewer output.


# 19. Git as Epistemic Version Control

Git is used for more than software-style file history.

Within this project, it should progressively support epistemic version control.

Important changes should make it possible to determine:

- what claim changed;
- why it changed;
- what evidence or reasoning caused the change;
- which other knowledge depends on it;
- who or what review process proposed the change;
- whether the change was approved;
- which version of the Knowledge System contains it.

Significant knowledge changes should therefore avoid silent overwriting.


# 20. Canonical Knowledge Principle

During migration, original DOCX files preserve the historical research baseline.

They must not be silently rewritten.

The intended future working architecture is based primarily on text-based, version-controllable canonical artifacts such as Markdown and structured YAML/JSON where appropriate.

However:

> conversion does not itself grant canonical status.

A migrated artifact becomes canonical only after the defined migration and fidelity-control process has been completed.


# 21. Preservation Before Improvement

The migration follows the sequence:

Preservation
→ Faithful Representation
→ Structural Normalization
→ Validation
→ Improvement

Migration and intellectual revision are separate operations.

If an error is discovered during migration, the preferred response is to register the issue rather than silently correct the migrated material.

This preserves provenance and makes later changes auditable.


# 22. Living Knowledge Principle

The Knowledge System is expected to evolve.

Revision is not considered evidence that previous research was useless.

Instead, mature knowledge should be capable of recording:

- what was previously believed;
- why it was believed;
- what challenged it;
- what changed;
- what survived;
- what remains unresolved.

The objective is not to create an immutable doctrine.

The objective is to create knowledge that can improve without losing its history.


# 23. Architecture Must Remain Revisable

The current repository, chapter, curriculum, and methodology architecture must not become immune to criticism merely because it was designed early.

Architecture itself is subject to:

- review;
- contradiction analysis;
- remove-the-concept tests;
- redundancy analysis;
- alternative architecture comparison;
- retrospective revalidation.

At the same time, architecture should not be changed casually.

Changes require a reason and should consider downstream effects.


# 24. Separation of Research Order and Reality Order

The sequence in which the project researches concepts does not establish a universal sequence in which Strategy must occur.

For example, an organization may:

- act before fully diagnosing;
- learn through action;
- discover Strategy retrospectively;
- revise objectives after new evidence;
- experience emergent strategic patterns.

The Knowledge System must therefore avoid turning its own educational architecture into an unsupported theory of organizational reality.


# 25. Anti-Confirmation-Bias Principle

One of the strongest project safeguards is separation between knowledge construction and organizational preference.

When later applying the system to a real organization, the preferred reasoning direction is:

Universal Knowledge
→ Organizational Facts and Evidence
→ Competing Interpretations
→ Candidate Strategic Implications
→ Critique
→ Decision

The system should actively detect the reverse pattern:

Desired Decision
→ Selective Evidence
→ Convenient Framework
→ Strategic Justification


# 26. Framework Philosophy

Strategic frameworks are tools, not Strategy.

A framework should be studied by asking:

- what question does it solve;
- what assumptions does it contain;
- what information does it require;
- what does it reveal;
- what does it hide;
- where does it fail;
- what decisions can it legitimately inform.

Framework completion must not be mistaken for strategic reasoning.


# 27. Educational Philosophy

The project should teach the learner to reason rather than memorize definitions.

Where appropriate, major concepts should support a learning structure such as:

Concept
→ Why
→ Distinction
→ Causal Mechanism
→ Strategic Question
→ Quality Criteria
→ Antipatterns
→ Approaches to Answering
→ Application Note
→ New Questions

The exact structure may evolve as the methodology develops.


# 28. Answer Quality

Future organizational answers should not be evaluated only as correct or incorrect.

Where useful, the system should make it possible to distinguish answer quality such as:

- weak;
- acceptable;
- strong;
- overly abstract or overloaded.

Quality criteria should help explain why one strategic answer is more useful or defensible than another.


# 29. Open Questions Are First-Class Knowledge

An unanswered question is not automatically a defect.

Open questions may identify:

- missing evidence;
- unresolved theoretical disagreement;
- required future research;
- architectural uncertainty;
- domain-specific dependency;
- intentionally deferred work.

Open questions should therefore be preserved and revisited systematically.


# 30. Intended Long-Term System

The long-term architecture may be understood approximately as:

Research Sources
↓
Evidence
↓
Claims
↓
Causal Mechanisms
↓
Concepts
↓
Relations
↓
Chapters

Cross-Chapter Knowledge System
↓
Knowledge Architecture

↓
Strategic Reasoning and Decisions

This model is provisional and remains subject to architecture review.


# 31. Expected Outputs

The project may eventually produce several outputs at different levels of compression.

Indicatively:

**Research Corpus**
Deep evidence and reasoning layer.

↓

**Strategy Knowledge Book**
Educational synthesis.

↓

**Strategy Playbook**
More compact decision and application layer.

↓

**Organizational Strategy Question Framework**
Structured bridge between knowledge and organizational strategy formulation.

These outputs serve different purposes and should not be collapsed into a single document.


# 32. Project Boundaries

The Strategy Knowledge System is not intended to become:

- a generic collection of business frameworks;
- a consulting-template library;
- an unquestioned summary of famous strategy authors;
- a repository of company-specific justifications;
- a collection of AI-generated essays;
- a static textbook;
- a substitute for organizational evidence;
- a mechanism for automatically generating Strategy.

The system should improve strategic reasoning, not replace it.


# 33. Current Migration Principle

The project is currently being transferred from a chat-centered and DOCX-centered working environment into a Git-based, Codex-compatible Knowledge System.

The migration must preserve the intellectual history of the existing work while separating:

- original research artifacts;
- reconstructed project context;
- current project state;
- methodology;
- structured knowledge;
- future revisions.

The old conversation should not remain a permanent hidden dependency of the project.


# 34. Definition of Successful Context Transfer

The project context has been transferred successfully when a new qualified researcher or AI agent, without access to the historical conversation, can understand:

- what the project is;
- why it exists;
- what epistemic standards govern it;
- how universal knowledge differs from organizational application;
- why research and review are separated;
- why provenance matters;
- why Git is used;
- why migration and revision are separate;
- what kinds of artifacts the system is intended to contain.

Understanding what has already been completed is a separate requirement handled by `PROJECT_STATE.md`.


# 35. Governing Principle

The project should optimize neither for maximum certainty nor maximum complexity.

It should optimize for:

> defensible, criticizable, traceable, revisable and decision-useful strategic knowledge.
